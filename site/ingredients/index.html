The corpus

191 distinct ingredients appear across 12 foundations, and 105 of them (54%) appear in exactly one product. Only 7 ingredients show up in three-quarters or more of them. Foundations are not one formula in twelve bottles — but they are built on a very small common spine.

Every product here carries an SPF claim, which in the United States makes it an over-the-counter drug. That matters for sourcing: the manufacturer must file the complete ingredient declaration with the FDA, and that filing is public. Nothing in this corpus comes from a retailer listing or a transcription site.

Selection is deliberately narrow: liquid, lotion, cream or emulsion foundations only. Powders, sticks and cushions use a different vehicle and would not be comparable. The cost of that rigour is a real bias — SPF-free foundations (Estée Lauder Double Wear, Maybelline Fit Me Matte + Poreless, Fenty Pro Filt'r) have no FDA filing and are excluded rather than sourced more weakly.

The common spine

These 7 ingredients appear in at least 75% of the corpus. They are the vehicle, the film former, the rheology package and the preservative — the part of a foundation that makes it a foundation at all.

What the labels themselves get wrong

An FDA filing is verbatim what the manufacturer submitted, typos included. Rimmel's label alone declares Butylene Glucol, Triethoxycaprylysilance, Hydroxyethyleclulose and Alpha-Isomethyl Lonone. Every correction this project makes is declared in tools/build_corpus.py and recorded per-product in the corpus, so a reader can see exactly what was changed and disagree with it. There are no silent edits.

Every figure on this page is generated from the corpus in the repo by the scripts in tools/, and traces to a primary filing. Read the disclaimer — nothing here is medical, dermatological or safety advice, and cost figures are estimates, not company data.
